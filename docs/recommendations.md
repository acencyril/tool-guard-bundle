# Public recommendations, and the Tool Guard feature for each

Three public texts say what should stand between an AI agent and the actions it can take. This page
maps what each one recommends to the Tool Guard feature that helps implement it, and says what Tool
Guard does **not** cover. Using Tool Guard does not make a system meet any of these texts on its own:
each asks for organisation, risk analysis, documentation and people, not only code.

Sources:

- **EU AI Act**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, [Article 14, Human oversight](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (EUR-Lex; the article alone on the Commission's [AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-14)). It applies to **high-risk** AI systems (as the regulation defines them), not to every agent.
- **ANSSI**, [*Recommandations de sécurité pour un système d'IA générative*](https://cyber.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/Recommandations_de_s%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_pour_un_syst%C3%A8me_d_IA_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9rative.pdf) (29 April 2024; the link now leads to the guide's page on messervices.cyber.gouv.fr; [English edition](https://cyber.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/security_recommandations_for_a_generative_ai_system.pdf)). 35 recommendations, R1 to R35; the titles below are those of the English edition.
- **OWASP GenAI Security Project**, [Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/) (9 December 2025), risks ASI01 to ASI10 and their mitigations.

## The map

| Source | What the text recommends | Tool Guard | What it covers, exactly |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI Act art. 14(4)(e) | Overseers can "intervene in the operation of the high-risk AI system or interrupt the system through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure that allows the system to come to a halt in a safe state" | **Stop button** (`tool-guard:stop`, approvals page, `StopButton`) | Halts the AI's tool calls (every agent, one agent, one tool) or, in an emergency, every caller; checked before any rule, across every PHP process. Safe state: a stopped call does not run (nothing half done) and held calls stay held, not lost. It stops the **actions**; the model itself keeps answering in text. |
| AI Act art. 14(4)(d) | Overseers can decide not to use the system, or "disregard, override or reverse" its output | `#[RequiresApproval]`, `#[Limit(above: Limit::APPROVAL)]`, Reject on the approvals page | A risky call waits; a human approves the exact arguments or rejects it. Reversing an action that already ran is the app's job. |
| AI Act art. 14(4)(a) | Overseers can monitor the operation, detect anomalies | The hash-chained journal, the approvals page, the profiler panel, `ToolCallRefused` / `ApprovalRequested` events, Notifier and ntfy alerts | Every guarded call, decision, stop and resume is recorded and can be watched. Detecting an anomaly is left to a person, or to your listener on the events. |
| AI Act art. 14(1), (3) | Oversight built in, with "appropriate human-machine interface tools", commensurate with the risks and the autonomy | Rules in attributes on each tool; `observe` mode to tune them; `debug:tool-guard --fail-on-unguarded` | Per-tool rules proportionate to the action (a 20 € refund alone, 500 € with a human, 5 000 € never). The risk analysis that sets those numbers is yours. |
| ANSSI R9 | "Do not allow AI systems to run automatically critical actions on the IS" | `#[RequiresApproval]`, `#[Limit]`, `#[Granted]` | A critical action is held for a human or refused, in code, whatever the model was told. Deciding which actions are critical is yours. |
| ANSSI R27 | "Limit automatic actions performed by an AI system handling uncontrolled inputs" | `#[Limit]`, `#[RateLimit]`, `max_pending_per_user`, agents' roles | Bounds what an agent can do on its own, how much and how often, and how many requests it can push to a human. |
| ANSSI R26, R35 | Check the authorisation of the AI system's access to business applications, not only authentication; review its rights regularly | `#[Granted]` with your Symfony voters (the tool argument as subject); `tool_guard.agents` (each agent's own role); `debug:tool-guard` | Least privilege per call: the user **and** the agent must both be allowed, on this order, this amount. The listing helps the regular review; holding the review is yours. |
| ANSSI R15 | "Provide a downgraded version of business services without an AI system" | "Stop the agents" keeps the same tools working for people and the app's own code (`Invocation::HUMAN`), with their rules | The business action goes on without the AI. Building the back-office form or workflow that calls it is yours. |
| ANSSI R29 (and R26) | "Record all processing carried out within the AI system", at the right granularity | `JsonlJournal`, hash-chained; `tool-guard:approvals verify <head>` | Tool calls, decisions, approvals, stops and resumes, tamper-evident. Not the prompts, the model's answers or the infrastructure logs. |
| OWASP ASI02 Tool Misuse and Exploitation | A policy enforcement point that validates intent and arguments, enforces rate limits; per-tool least privilege; human confirmation for high-impact or destructive actions; logs of every tool invocation | `Guard` on every tool call (agent, MCP, `GuardedInvoker`), `#[Limit]`, `#[RateLimit]`, `#[RequiresApproval]`, the journal | Arguments checked in code before the tool runs (missing, not a number, infinite, out of range: refused). No schema validation beyond numbers, no network egress control. |
| OWASP ASI03 Identity and Privilege Abuse | Narrowly scoped rights per task; human approval for high-privilege or irreversible actions | Agents' roles, `#[Granted]`, approvals with `four_eyes`, the requester never approves his own call | Neither the agent nor its user gets more than both may do. Short-lived credentials and token handling are not covered. |
| OWASP ASI10 Rogue Agents (and ASI04) | Kill switches and rapid mechanisms to disable an agent, a tool or a connection at once | **Stop button**: one agent, one tool, every agent, or everything | Takes effect at the next call, in every process, without a deploy. Revoking the agent's credentials elsewhere (API keys, OAuth tokens) is not covered. |
| OWASP ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack, ASI09 Human-Agent Trust Exploitation | Human approval for high-impact actions; do not let the agent's words persuade the approver | Held calls show the policy's reason and the raw arguments, never text the model wrote; approval on another channel | A hijacked agent cannot approve itself or write its own justification on the approver's screen. Detecting the hijack in the prompt is not covered. |
| OWASP ASI08 Cascading Failures | Quotas, circuit breakers between planner and executor | `#[RateLimit]` (per user or per tool), `max_pending_per_user`, a workflow stops at the first refused or held step (`ToolCallBlocked`) | Bounds the damage of a loop on guarded tools. |

## What Tool Guard does not cover

- **The model and its prompts**: no prompt-injection detection, no input or output filtering (ANSSI R25), no jailbreak detection. Tool Guard assumes the model can be talked into anything and guards what it can *do*.
- **Unguarded paths**: a tool reached another way than through a Symfony AI toolbox, an MCP server built with symfony/mcp-bundle or the PHP MCP SDK, or `GuardedInvoker` is not guarded. `debug:tool-guard --fail-on-unguarded` lists the AI tools without rules, not code paths around them.
- **Reversal**: an action that ran is not undone by a stop or a rejection. Idempotency (not doing an action twice after a stop, a resume or a retry) belongs to the service that executes it.
- **Infrastructure and lifecycle**: hosting, isolation, supply chain, training data, model files, secrets, audits and tests before production (ANSSI R1 to R8, R10 to R24, R28, R30 to R34; OWASP ASI04 supply chain, ASI05 code execution, ASI06 memory poisoning, ASI07 inter-agent communication) are outside a tool guard.
- **The legal and organisational part**: risk classification, technical documentation, the training and authority of the people doing the oversight (AI Act art. 14(4)(a) to (c) are about what those people understand), conformity assessment. Tool Guard gives them the controls, not the role.
- **Remote biometric identification** (AI Act art. 14(5), two people verifying each identification) is a different use; `four_eyes` only says the requester cannot approve their own call.
